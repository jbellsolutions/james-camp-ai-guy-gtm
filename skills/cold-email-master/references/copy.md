# Truthful and relevant copy

Write like a clear person speaking to another person. Brief, concrete subject; no fake reply/forward markers or false familiarity. One source-backed reason this recipient is relevant. Explain one customer problem and one offer/mechanism plainly. End with a small, clear next step and an easy way to decline. Prefer a reply as the first goal when that matches the approved strategy.

Initial cold messages generally fit on a phone screen; a house drafting target is 60–100 words, with shorter follow-ups. Treat those as review targets, not universal provider requirements. Simple vocabulary and short sentences; no unsupported statistics, invented results, quotes, testimonials, shared experiences or timeline promises. Never claim you personally performed something the agent did not do.

Personalization should survive the question “Why this person?” A location and industry alone usually provide weak relevance. If research is thin, use an honest segment-level approach approved for that campaign or hold the recipient; never invent a detail. Cite the source behind every personalization fact and approved proof claim in the review artifact, rather than cluttering the recipient’s message.

Follow-ups add a distinct helpful reason to answer; they do not nag or pretend a relationship exists. Respect a no, not-now, complaint and opt-out. Signature/sender/links/footer must match the approved identity and market requirements. No unresolved template tokens in rendered messages. Test both text/HTML and every chosen variable before staging.

Editorial independently checks exact renders: truthful claims, personalization provenance, current offer, tone, CTA, signature, links/footer, substitution, formatting and reading clarity. Its PASS names the artifact hash; any material edit invalidates it. Mechanical QC never certifies that a claim is true.
